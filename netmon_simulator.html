    Limitations to state in the viva

    • Fixed thresholds cannot remove both error types. Raising util_high/cpu_high silences the benign backup (FP) but makes more attacks like the slow attack invisible (FN); lowering them does the opposite.
    • The rules see numbers, not intent. A legitimate 8 Mb/s backup at 85% CPU is indistinguishable from a flood.
    • CPU is staged with --cpu-override, because all Mininet hosts share the VM's CPU. The override keeps reporting 92%/85% after iperf stops, which is why CPU_SATURATION fires near the end of the abnormal and backup scenarios.
    • UDP telemetry is unreliable and can be forged; sequence numbers reveal loss but not forgery. If telemetry stops, HOST_FLOOD can no longer fire for that host.
    • One switch, one topology, a few runs: results are indicative, not statistical. The system detects and reports; it does not block traffic.

    What is real in the project and what is simulated here

    • Same rules and constants as monitor_controller.py: POLL_INTERVAL 2 s, telemetry every 2 s, STALE_AFTER 6 s, MIN_BPS 50 kb/s, CONFIRM_CYCLES 2, COOLDOWN 10 s, LINK_CAPACITY 10 Mb/s, util_high 70%, util_low 20%, cpu_high 80%, mismatch_ratio 50%, mismatch_min 0.5 Mb/s. Rates are computed the same way: rate = Δbytes × 8 ÷ Δt per flow, telemetry flows (udp_dst 9999) are skipped, new flows use their own age as the baseline.
    • Same scenario timing as topology.py: telemetry starts at t = 0, baseline iperf at ≈1 s, the event under test at ≈16 s (the start mark), iperf lasts 30 s, the run ends 35 s (or 30 s) later.
    • Simulated, deterministic: byte counters grow at the iperf rate plus UDP/IP/Ethernet headers (1512 bytes on the wire per 1470-byte datagram). A sender is limited to 10 Mb/s by its link; the switch counts packets toward a full link before they are dropped, which is why downlink utilisation can exceed 100%. Memory is the shared VM value (the same for every host).
    • Not simulated: real CPU load, packet loss of telemetry, ARP/ping traffic, the Mininet CLI. Nothing here talks to a real network.
